# Film Bible — "The Forger Who Confessed" (Han van Meegeren)

**Format:** vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps. **Voice:** `audio/clean.wav`, 75.92 s, 174 words, 33 lines, all times in `audio/clean.json` / `audio/lines.json` (film time = the voice's clock; t = 0 is the first sample).
**Source:** Araby's own voiceover + his word-timed transcript, prepared 2026-11-26. Lines are written **as heard** (Egyptian dialect kept: ما لقاش = he found no way, نصاب = a fraud/swindler, مخدعة = had fooled, ما حدش يصدقك = nobody believes you). Nothing in the read differs from the story he described; the history lands in the same order he tells it: the trap (a treason charge, death penalty) → the arrest over a "Vermeer" found with Göring → the confession nobody believed ("I painted them myself" — the experts had certified the fakes 100% real) → the proof (a new "Vermeer" painted under guard, and lab analysis finding chemicals that didn't exist in Vermeer's century) → the verdict (treason dropped, one year for forgery, 1947 — a criminal, but of a different kind).

**Casting decision (binding):** one lead, **Han van Meegeren himself** — the narrator-hero (`who: 'hero'`) — is on screen for the whole film and says every line himself, storytime staging: he tells his own story to camera from wherever the scene stands (the interrogation chair, his studio, the dock). Reason: the kit's first rule is an expressive face in frame 0 acting every word, and this narration is built on his face — terror, defiance, wounded pride, the grin at the end. Lines 12–13 are his *quoted* confession and land hardest in his own mouth ("أنا اللي راسمها بإيدي"). Everyone else (interrogators, guards, experts, the judge, Göring) is a **supporting figure** the line calls for — present, reactive, never the speaker. Göring appears once, from behind / in silhouette at his painting (line 6–7): a uniformed back, never a face, never glorified.

## Colour script

- **Interrogation (beats 1, 3):** desaturated blue-grey and brown; one hard warm lamp pool. Fear and irony play in the coldest light in the film.
- **The arrest / collection (beat 2):** the salon's bottle-green and gold — treasure gloom; the painting glows warmer than the room, which is exactly the problem.
- **Studio (beat 4):** golden ochre and umber, north-window daylight — Vermeer's palette, the film's warmest and richest light; the forger at home in the master's colours.
- **Lab (beat 4):** cold clinical white and glass-green — the modern world answering the golden lie; one colour-change vial as the beat's warm event.
- **Courtroom (beat 5):** solemn oak and pale gold shafts; relief and sentence in the same light — the verdict doesn't change the weather, only the words.
- One film, five places, five looks — with the sound off you always know where you are.

## Risks flagged at prep (for the Build pass and Araby)

- **Arabic captions — solved once, re-prove here:** captions are `words` style, burned in by the engine. The supercool pilot proved the fix: the harness registers `NotoSansArabic-Black.ttf` via CDP under the engine's font names ('Segoe UI Black' / 'Arial Black', Arabic ranges only), no engine edit. The same harness + font are copied into this project — the pilot must still re-prove Arabic shaping on THIS film's frame 0 before any full build.
- **Bidi / digits in captions (new in this film):** the narration speaks Latin-script tokens inside Arabic lines — "1945", "1947", "100%" (lines 4, 16, 29). Digits and the % sign inside RTL word captions can reorder or render mirrored; the pilot must check lines 4, 16 and 29 captions word by word. The names (هان فان ميجرين، هيرمان جورينج، فيرمير) are spoken and captioned in Arabic script as heard — no Latin name captions needed.
- **Painting-within-painting readability:** THE PAINTING is the film's second actor and must read at phone size as a golden Dutch interior *and* as the same canvas across three places and two ages (aged/cracked vs fresh/wet). It needs one dedicated painted texture reused everywhere (never redrawn per shot), with craquelure and the stamp marks as timed overlays. If the texture reads muddy at 1080 wide, the whole story's object is lost — prove it in the pilot at lines 5, 15 and 22.
- **Period props and places:** 1940s Dutch interiors, a police tunic, a courtroom — the kit's prop library is modern-leaning; expect custom work in `scenes/props/` and Blender set dressing (easel, microscope, gilt frames, shuttered windows). Budget the Environment pass accordingly.
- **Göring / Nazi imagery (sensitivity + platform policy):** Göring is staged from behind, in silhouette, once — no face, no swastika or insignia close-ups anywhere in the film (uniforms read by cut and colour only). This keeps the film inside YouTube/TikTok policy on extremist content in an educational/historical context; flagged here so no pass "improves" him into view.
- **A bigger cast than the reference film:** supercool carried one character; this film has a hero plus five supporting figures. They are deliberately simple (silhouettes, backs, one-scene reactors), but Build must cast them cheaply and keep Han's face the subject of every shot — faces rule: within ~60° of camera, big, never edge-cut.
- **Five places, one 76-second film:** the interrogation room carries beats 1 and 3 (the confession is the film's centre); the other four places are short visits. Sets must be built to the set bar even for 3-line stays — the collection (3 lines) and lab (4 lines) are the cost risk; keep their geometry shared where honest (dark hall / bench room shells) and spend the detail where the camera looks.
- End: last word ends 75.90; the engine's automatic ending (~0.5 s after the last word/action) puts the film end at ≈ 76.4 s. `project.json` end is the voice end (75.92), as in the reference project.
